# NeuroFly: pipeline video, voice-over script (EN)

Video: `data/videos/neurofly_pipeline.mp4`, 74 s, 1920×1080. Read at a calm pace (about 2.5 words per second). Each block starts when its tile lights up.

## 00:00 to 00:06  Intro

> What if a real fruit fly brain could fly a drone? This is NeuroFly.

On screen: *The brain of a fruit fly pilots a drone*

## 00:06 to 00:14  Tile 1: Camera

> It starts with a camera. Two small cameras on the drone's nose watch the world, thirty frames every second.

On screen: *Two cameras on the drone's nose, 30 frames a second*

## 00:14 to 00:22  Tile 2: Fly eye

> Each frame becomes a fly's compound eye: seven hundred and twenty-one tiny facets per eye, and each one sees only brightness.

On screen: *721 facets per eye, each sees only brightness*

## 00:22 to 00:32  Tile 3: FlyVis

> FlyVis, a published model of the fly's visual system, turns those facets into the signals of real fly visual neurons, including its motion detectors.

On screen: *A published model of the fly's visual system*

## 00:32 to 00:45  Tile 4: Fly brain

> Those signals enter the fly brain itself: the BANC connectome. One hundred seventy-five thousand real neurons and one and a half million connections, mapped synapse by synapse, simulated on a laptop GPU.

On screen: *175,401 real neurons from the BANC connectome*

## 00:45 to 00:56  Tile 5: Decoder

> Flight commands leave the brain through three hundred seventy-five descending neurons. Our decoder reads them one by one and turns them into four numbers: thrust, roll, pitch and yaw.

On screen: *375 flight neurons become four numbers*

## 00:56 to 01:08  Tile 6: Drone

> The drone moves, its eyes see a new picture, and the loop starts again. Near the target, every turn comes from the fly brain. Far away, GPS helps.

On screen: *It moves, sees a new picture, and the loop starts again*

## 01:08 to 01:14  Outro

> Evolution wrote the wiring. We gave it wings. NeuroFly.

On screen: *We gave it wings.*
